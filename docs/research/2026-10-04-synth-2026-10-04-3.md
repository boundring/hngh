## Findings

### 1. Claim Assessment: "Inert by Construction"

The identity's claim that `accept-plans.py` admission renders malformed synth output inert by construction is **partially supported but not fully validated** by available evidence.

- The `accept-plans.py` admission layer does perform structural validation on synth output before it enters the execution pipeline, which would theoretically prevent malformed plans from being executed.
- However, the 2026-09-14 occurrence demonstrates that a malformed synth output was still produced and consumed, even if it was subsequently discarded. The key distinction: **inert by construction** implies the malformed output is never generated or processed, whereas the evidence shows it was generated and processed before admission rejection occurred.

### 2. Budget Burn Analysis

The 2026-09-14 event burned the day's synth budget despite being a single-shot discard. This indicates:

- Synth budget allocation occurs **before** admission validation, meaning the cost of generating the malformed output was incurred regardless of downstream rejection.
- This creates a **budget inefficiency** where resources are consumed to produce output that admission would reject.
- The budget burn is not prevented by the admission layer's inertness guarantee, because the generation cost precedes admission.

### 3. Structural Relationship

The relationship between generation, admission, and budget is:

```
Synth Generation (costs budget) → Admission Validation (accepts/rejects) → Execution
```

The admission layer's "inert by construction" property applies only to the **execution** phase, not to the **generation** phase where budget is consumed.

## Recommendations

### 1. Budget-Aware Generation

Implement budget-aware generation that can detect admission-rejection risk before full output generation completes. This would prevent budget burn on outputs that admission would reject.

**Reference:** Prior art `[[concepts/budget-aware-delegation]]` (created: 2026-08-24) suggests this pattern exists in the system.

### 2. Admission Feedback Loop

Create a feedback mechanism where admission rejection signals propagate back to the generation phase, allowing early termination of generation for outputs that will be rejected.

### 3. Budget Accounting Reform

Reform budget accounting to distinguish between generation costs and execution costs, making it clear that admission rejection does not refund generation costs.

### 2. Budget Allocation Timing

The timing of budget allocation relative to admission validation needs clarification. If budget is allocated at generation start rather than on a per-step basis, the budget inefficiency identified here is systemic rather than incidental.

## Conclusion

The identity's claim that `accept-plans.py` admission "makes malformed synth output inert by construction" is **misleading** in its implication that budget burn is prevented. The admission layer does prevent malformed output from execution, but it does not prevent the budget cost of generating that output. The 2026-09-14 single-shot discard confirms this: the output was generated (burning budget) and then rejected (preventing execution). The line is now contracted and finalized.
