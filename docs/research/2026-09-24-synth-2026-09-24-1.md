## 1. Findings (what the line established)

**F1. The recurring failure pattern is a missing *feedback edge*, not a missing threshold.**
Across the prior art (notably [[sources/LES-fail-20260915-What-are-the-exact-file-paths-for-the-ca]], [[sources/LES-fail-20260915-If-drift-is-confirmed-in-the-scroll-beha]], [[sources/LES-fail-20260915-If-R1-confirms-1-surviving-class-on-1-ho]]), the recurring shape is: a guardrail observes a condition, a threshold exists that should distinguish "steer" (course-correct, continue) from "die" (halt/kill the line or host process), but the **outcome of the decision is not written back into the ledger** in a form the next decision point reads. The loop is open at the ledger-write or ledger-read edge. (Inference from lesson-note titles and the line question; the underlying code was not readable this transition.)

**F2. Two schemas must be verified, and they are different things.**
The vault distinguishes an **authority/evidence ledger** ([[concepts/evidence-ledger]], [[sources/SRC-2026-08-24-021]] — an *external design source* whose claims I cannot verify beyond the vault's summary) from the **research line state** (`research-lines.tsv`, confirmed to exist as the line-state file per the task prompt). The recurring failure pattern appears to conflate them: guardrail outcomes need a row in an *operational* ledger (what was decided, when, by which guardrail, with what threshold reading), while the line's lifecycle state (`contracting`, etc.) lives in the TSV. Closing the loop requires the guardrail decision record to exist *independently* of line state, so that a dead line still leaves a legible corpse.

**F3. The guardrail wiring has four verification points.**
From the failure lessons, the steer-vs-die decision must be checked at:
1. **Sense** — the metric the guardrail reads (drift signal, host liveness, harvest staleness) is actually the metric the threshold was calibrated against. ([[sources/LES-fail-20260915-If-drift-is-confirmed-in-the-scroll-beha]] is the relevant lesson.)
2. **Compare** — the threshold constant lives in exactly one place and the guardrail reads it from there, not from a copy. ([[sources/LES-fail-20260915-What-are-the-exact-file-paths-for-the-ca]] — the lesson is literally about exact paths being the failure site.)
3. **Act** — the "die" branch actually terminates what it claims to terminate (lesson [[sources/LES-fail-20260915-If-R1-confirms-1-surviving-class-on-1-ho]] is the surviving-process lesson).
4. **Record** — the decision (steer or die), the threshold reading, and the host/line identity are appended to the operational ledger *before* the action, so a crash mid-action still leaves the decision legible.

**F4. The ledger schema, minimal form (proposed, unverified against repo).**
The operational decision record needs, at minimum: `ts`, `host`, `line_id`, `guardrail_id`, `metric_name`, `metric_value`, `threshold_value`, `decision ∈ {steer, die}`, `action_result ∈ {ok, partial, failed, unverified}`. The field `action_result = unverified` is the loop-closing addition: it forces the next reader to confirm the die actually died (F3 point 3). **This schema is the line's proposal, not a claim about what exists.**
