## Findings

**F1 (mechanism, logically implied; file-level confirmation pending).** A whitelist (`hngh_wl`) that silences tree-skew false alarms works by telling the monitor to exclude certain paths from the skew comparison. If the plan ledger's path is a member of that whitelist — added because the ledger is written frequently out-of-band and therefore always "skews" against the last committed tree — then the monitor stops looking at the ledger entirely. Whitelisting is not alarm suppression at the reporting layer; it is exclusion at the comparison layer. The side effect is total invisibility, not just quiet.

**F2 (consequence, implied by the line title's "open finding").** Because the ledger is unversioned (not committed into the tracked tree), there is no second channel by which its state changes reach oversight. Whitelist exclusion removes the monitor's view; absence from version control removes the tree's view. The two gaps compose: `oversight-tick.sh` sees neither the file nor its history. The ledger's status as "first item for the Audit station" in `design/descent.md` is consistent with it being the known worst case of this double-blindness.

## Recommendations

**R1 — Minimal versioning change (primary recommendation).** Track the plan ledger in version control at a stable path, but keep it *out of the skew comparison via a deliberately typed exemption, not via `hngh_wl`*:

- Commit the ledger (append-only) on each plan mutation, or on a bounded cadence.
- Replace the `hngh_wl` blanket whitelist entry with a narrower predicate: exempt *this specific path* from tree-skew diffing while still surfacing its existence, size, mtime, and last-commit-hash to the monitor.
- Then `oversight-tick.sh` can assert "ledger changed without a corresponding commit" — an alarm the current setup cannot even express.

This is minimal in that it changes (a) one file's versioned status and (b) one whitelist entry's semantics, and nothing else in the monitor.

**R2 — If versioning the ledger is rejected** (e.g., it's a high-frequency scratch surface), the fallback is: keep it unversioned but remove it from `hngh_wl`, and give it a dedicated lightweight check in `oversight-tick.sh` (exists? non-empty? mtime within bound?). This trades tree-skew flap risk for explicit, bounded oversight. Not recommended as the default, since R1's commit-on-mutation is cheap for an append-only ledger.

**R3 — Flap safety.** Whichever option is taken, pin the ledger's *comparison unit* to something coarse (commit hash or content hash), not to timestamp-level tree diffing. Tree-skew flaps arise when the comparator is sensitive to the very writes the ledger exists to record; hashing decouples "the ledger was written" from "the tree is skewed."

**R4 — Whitelist hygiene generally.** `hngh_wl` entries should record: *what* is exempted, *from which check*, *why*, and *what compensating visibility exists*. A whitelist entry with no compensating check is a blind spot by construction. The ledger is the first discovered instance; the Audit station should sweep the whole whitelist for others.
