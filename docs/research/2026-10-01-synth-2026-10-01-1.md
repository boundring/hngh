## Findings

### F1. The steer-vs-die decision is currently ad hoc, and the inputs for a rule exist
The prior material establishes the components of a threshold rule but no rule itself. The vault contains: a salvage concept ([[concepts/session-salvage]]), a drift-confirmation failure lesson ([[sources/LES-fail-20260915-If-drift-is-confirmed-in-the-scroll-beha]]), a steering synthesis ([[syntheses/delegated-subagent-steering]]), and a budget-burn finding ([[sources/session-budget-burn-prevents-discretionary-plan-selection]]). No vault note in the record defines a numeric or structural threshold. The gap this line was chartered to close is real.

### F2. Budget burn is the dominant cost asymmetry (basis finding)
From [[sources/session-budget-burn-prevents-discretionary-plan-selection]] ("Bad-execution burn exhausts…"): execution attempts consume the discretionary budget that would otherwise fund plan *selection*. Steering a stalled session is not free — it spends from the same pool the session would need to adopt a better plan after the steer. This creates the core asymmetry the rule must encode: **a late steer is strictly worse than an early kill**, because the steered session can lack the residual budget to act on the steering.

### F3. Drift must be *confirmed against an observable behavior*, not inferred
From [[sources/LES-fail-20260915-If-drift-is-confirmed-in-the-scroll-beha]]: the recorded failure lesson conditions action on drift being confirmed in a concrete behavior (in that case, scroll behavior). A stall is ambiguous — it may be productive latency, blocked waiting, or genuine derailment. The rule therefore needs a confirmation step keyed to an observable signal before either steering or killing, not a timeout alone.

### F4. Steering authority can be delegated, and delegation changes who applies the rule
From [[syntheses/delegated-subagent-steering]]: steering is itself a delegable activity. The threshold rule must therefore be stated so a supervising context can apply it to a subordinate session, and must specify what the supervisor observes (progress signals, budget ledger) rather than relying on the stalled session's self-report.

### F5. Salvage is a third outcome, but a bounded one
From [[concepts/session-salvage]]: sessions can be salvaged (partial state carried forward) rather than continued or killed. Salvage subsumes neither steer nor die; it is what "die" should mean when the session produced durable artifacts. The rule's kill branch should default to salvage-then-restart, preserving verified output.

### F6. Meta-finding: this line's own beats exhibited the failure mode
The prior beat spent 100.0s wall on exploration and produced no synthesis. A steer-vs-die rule applied to research beats would have killed or redirected that beat. This is anecdotal (n=1) but consistent with F2.

## Recommendation: the steer-vs-die threshold rule

**Proposed rule (parameters require calibration — see O1):**

For a session judged stalled, evaluate in order:

1. **Confirm drift.** If no concrete behavioral divergence from task intent is observable (per F3), the session is *waiting*, not stalled — do nothing; re-check at the next observation point.
2. **Budget-reserve test.** Estimate remaining discretionary budget. If steering plus acting-on-the-steer would leave less than a reserve fraction **R** for subsequent plan selection (per F2), go to step 4. **Proposed R ≈ 30%, uncalibrated.**
3. **Steer-count test.** If the session has already absorbed **K** steers with no measurable progress signal since the last one, do not steer again — go to step 4. Otherwise **steer**, log the steer and the expected progress signal, and reset the observation clock. **Proposed K = 2, uncalibrated.**
4. **Kill with salvage.** Terminate the session, carry forward verified artifacts per [[concepts/session-salvage]], and restart or escalate with the salvage as input.

Key properties relative to the prior ad hoc practice: the decision is order-dependent and deterministic given the observations; "die" is the default once either budget or steer budget is exhausted, which inverts the current bias toward indefinite steering; and the rule is applicable by a supervisor over subagents (per F4) because all inputs are externally observable.
