## Final Structured Summary

### Core Claim

A dream brief's citation of a completion-graph node must be a verifiable claim — identity + observed state + resolution policy — and budget accounting under P6/P7 should fall out of the same citation-resolution pass. Grounding and budgeting are not two checks; they are one traversal.

### Findings

1. **Citation bareness is the problem:** Current dream briefs reference nodes positionally or by name without pinning state, making them impossible to verify against the completion graph at proposal time.

2. **Budget and grounding are coupled:** The cost of a proposal depends on which nodes are cited and their resolution status. A stale citation isn't just ungrounded — it's more expensive.

3. **One-pass evaluation is necessary:** Separate budget and grounding checks invite drift between "what the brief claims" and "what the brief costs."

4. **Rehearsal is governance, not mutation:** Rehearsal beats run governance loops without mutating the completion graph, providing a safe way to test citation resolution.

### Recommendations

**R1 — Adopt the citation triple; reject bare node references**  
Every citation in a dream brief should be `(node_id, observed_state_hash, resolution_policy)`:
- **node_id** must be content-addressed or versioned — never positional
- **observed_state_hash** pins the node's completion status and metadata at draft time
- **resolution_policy** ∈ {`re-resolve`, `reject-if-stale`, `degrade-to-unverified`}

`degrade-to-unverified` is the important one: it applies the research-transition grounding rule inside the brief format. A stale citation does not silently stay grounded; it is demoted to an unverified assertion and costed accordingly.

**R2 — Compute the budget as the cost of the cited subgraph's closure**  
A brief's work envelope = cited nodes + their unresolved dependencies. Accounting rules:
- Brief ceiling = Σ cost attribution over the closure
- A citation that re-resolves to a **completed** node contributes zero marginal cost but still counts as evidence
- An unresolved or stale citation is costed at its full subtree estimate — staleness gets *more expensive*, never less

This makes "stay grounded" the cost-minimizing strategy rather than a compliance burden.

**R3 — One-pass evaluation in hngh-automation**  
Implement citation resolution as a single evaluation step that emits both artifacts: (a) the verified evidence base with per-citation freshness verdicts, (b) the budget envelope from R2. This belongs in the hngh-automation proposal-evaluation pipeline.

**R4 — Rehearse before accept**  
Rehearsal beats run governance loops without mutating the completion graph, providing a safe way to test citation resolution before committing to a proposal.

**R5 — Version the completion graph alongside briefs**  
The completion graph must be versioned so that citations can be resolved against a specific snapshot. This prevents the "which version of the graph?" problem when proposals are evaluated at different times.
