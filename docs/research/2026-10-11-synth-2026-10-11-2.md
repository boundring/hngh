## Final Findings

### R1 — Audit Classification Rules

Three concrete recommendations emerged from the `fail-20260916-patrol-handoffs` artifact:

1. **Handoff-integrity-based classification.** The failure mode where patrol state transitions between agents lose provenance continuity is not captured by generic severity levels. Classification should be keyed to handoff integrity status.

2. **Voice-rule implementation.** Per the prior-art pointer `[[sources/voice-rules-as-binding-constraint-not-aesthetic-preference]]`, operational constraints in hngh are enforced through voice rules that cannot be overridden by aesthetic preference. R1 must therefore be implementable as a constraint grammar, not a policy document.

3. **Provenance continuity invariant.** The receiving agent's context window must contain the full provenance of the outgoing agent's decisions. Violation produces non-actionable audit trails.

### R2 — Scheduling/Resource Measurement Requirements

Three concrete recommendations emerged:

1. **Context-transfer cost as first-class scheduling dimension.** The prior-art pointer `[[sources/obs-2026-08-24-omp-context-via-billion-context-proxy-hngh-attestation-desig]]` suggests hngh already has architecture for context transfer. R2 must measure not just compute time but the bandwidth and latency of moving audit-relevant state between patrol instances.

2. **Cost-aware scheduling for recalibration events.** A scheduling rule that ignores context-transfer cost will produce audit gaps during high-throughput handoffs.

3. **Evidence ledger piggyback.** Per `[[sources/SRC-2026-08-24-021]]`, if the evidence ledger is extensible to patrol-specific audit events, R1 classification can piggyback on this ledger mechanism for storage and retrieval.
